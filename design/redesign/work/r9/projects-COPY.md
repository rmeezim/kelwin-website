# Project pages: copy for review (round 9)

Five more project pages, written to the template Email deliverability is built on. This round they are copy
only; once you say yes, each is built as a mockup. All six are proposed, and each page carries one plain status
line: "Proposed — for the founder's yes".

**The template, said once here**
1. **Head.** An idea kicker, a two-voice h1 cast for that page, a lede of at most two lines, one quiet "Ask for
   this project", then the plate: pages from the document the client keeps.
2. **Who it's for.** The buyer's situation, set large, then the right project and the wrong one.
3. **The scope.** What is in and what is not — the page's one ruled list.
4. **What it takes.** Short prose, written from that project's own facts.
5. **Where it leads.** What it becomes inside the system, and one quiet destination.
6. **The close.** A door line of 12 words or fewer, the red "Request a System Audit", the quiet "Ask for this
   project" (→ /contact?topic=project&ref=<slug>) and "All projects".

**What no project page repeats**
- **Price and length.** The index's written-scope page shows them as blanks, agreed before work starts. No project
  page states them, or a rule about them.
- **The written stop after week one.** The index says it, and Email deliverability says it for sending. Below, a
  page mentions stopping only where that project has its own reason to stop.
- **Sentences.** No sentence, door line or "wrong project" line is shared between any two of the six pages. The
  door strings ("Request a System Audit", "Ask for this project") repeat on purpose.

**Rules this copy keeps:** no prices, no lengths as fact, no proof, no client names, no codes. Specimens hold
structure or procedure only, and say "Specimen" once, in a running head. New lines are marked [N]; a line quoted
from an approved page is marked [K] with its source.

**Who it's for**
- Statement: Five people describe the company five ways. [N]
- The right project: A raise, a launch or the first sales hire is close, and every message still goes through the founder.
- The wrong project: A logo, a site or a campaign is a brand studio's work. Deals stalling on the wrong accounts as well point to the System Audit.

**The scope**
- Claim: Whoever sells writes it with us. [N]
- Big number beside the list: **3** listeners (the champion, the buyer, the skeptic) — the section's one large object.
- In:
  - Interviews with customers and the people who sell

  - Brand identity, logo or site design
  - Moving the site, deck and sequences onto it
  - Testing which version lands: that needs the system's loop
  - Campaigns

**What it takes**

It starts with conversations: a handful of customers, and everyone who sells. The founder sits in the working
sessions, because the founder's version is the one buyers have heard so far. Customers' calendars set the pace.

## 2 · ICP and signal map · /projects/icp-and-signal-map

**Head**
- Kicker: Before buying another list
- h1: The accounts / to call this week, / *and why.* [N]
- Lede: Your ideal customer, written from closed-won deals, with accounts tiered inside your CRM and a pipeline that refreshes them. [N]
- Action: Ask for this project
- Plate (two pages of the map):
  - Page 4, "Tiers, and how an account moves": three tiers, each with the one rule that moves an account up or down.
  - Page 9, "How a signal expires", as an exhibit with the columns Signal · Comes from · Expires after. Its values
    are procedure settings, written as such, never results:

    | Signal | Comes from | Expires after |
    |---|---|---|
    | A sales-leadership or RevOps role opens | Your data provider | 60 days |
    | A second person from the account visits pricing | Your site analytics | 14 days |
    | Funding announced | A news feed | 90 days |

**Who it's for**
- Statement: Reps pick accounts by habit. [N]
- The right project: The ICP is a slide nobody opens, and the CRM holds a few quarters of closed-won deals to build from.
- The wrong project: We don't build, buy or sell lists; names to work through come cheaper from a data vendor. If nobody works the accounts once they're found, the gap is routing, which comes only with the system.

**The scope**
- Claim: The map comes from your own deals, not a vendor's idea of your market. [N] (the page's one antithesis)
- In:
  - One market
  - Your ICP, written from closed-won history
  - Accounts tiered inside your CRM
  - Signal sources chosen and connected, licensed in your name
  - The pipeline that refreshes the map, and its owner trained
- Not in:
  - A list or contact export
  - Anyone working the accounts, or sending
  - Routing: the Orchestration Rules come only with the system
  - Data-provider subscriptions

**What it takes**

The first week reads what your CRM already records about the deals you won. Some CRMs can't carry a map yet;
when yours can't, the week-one note names what's missing and the build waits for it. Bring CRM admin access and
the data tools you already pay for.

**Where it leads**
- In the full system the routing rules read from this map, and its signals are re-scored every quarter.
- Link (.ac-go): The GTM Infrastructure layer →

**Close**
- Line: A bought list starts ageing the day it arrives. (9 words)

## 3 · CRM blueprint · /projects/crm-blueprint

**Head**
- Kicker: Before the next forecast
- h1: One record / the *board* trusts. [N]
- Lede: A written blueprint for your CRM, decided with your admin, with the clean-up and decay rules running in the instance. [N]
- Action: Ask for this project
- Plate (two pages of the blueprint — not the stages page, which the GTM Infrastructure page already shows):
  - Page 11, "Dedupe and decay", an exhibit with the columns Rule · Runs · Owner (for example: two contacts share
    an email: the newer record wins · nightly · your admin; a contact silent for twelve months is marked stale ·
    monthly · your admin). Procedure settings, not results.
  - Page 12, "Who owns what": objects and automations by role, never by name.

**Who it's for**
- Statement: The board's number and the CRM's number disagree. [N]
- The right project: The forecast is rebuilt in a spreadsheet every month, a migration has just happened, or RevOps is about to be hired. Your admin or RevOps lead decides the blueprint with us and runs it after.
- The wrong project: Moving to a new CRM vendor is scoped on its own. If the pipeline is thin rather than miscounted, no blueprint will fill it.

**The scope**
- Claim: Each stage ends on something the buyer did. [N]
- In:
  - One instance, and the integrations named in your scope
  - The record read before anything changes
  - Objects, stages with exit criteria, required fields by stage
  - Clean-up, dedupe and decay rules, automated
  - Built in a sandbox, then live, with your admin trained
- Not in:
  - Migrating between CRM vendors
  - Integrations outside the scope, and tool licences
  - Dashboards
  - Policing data entry after handover

**What it takes**

We read the record as it stands, two quarters of stage changes included, before touching it. Everything is built
in a sandbox first; your admin needs one, along with admin access and a seat in the working sessions. A heavily
customised instance needs more time, and the first call is where we say so.

**Where it leads**
- It becomes the record under both infrastructure assets: an install builds the signal map and the routing rules into it.
- Link (.ac-go): The GTM Infrastructure layer →

**Close**
- Line: A forecast is only as honest as the stages under it. (11 words)

## 4 · Win/loss interviews · /projects/win-loss-interviews

**Head**
- Kicker: After a quarter of surprises
- h1: Why the deal was lost, / in the *buyer's* words. [R capability-services/U, recast]
- Lede: One round of conversations with people who recently bought from you or chose not to, written up so your team can run the next. [N]
- Action: Ask for this project
- Plate (two pages of the write-up):
  - Page 2, "Why deals moved": the section's structure only (won, lost, stalled), no buyer quotes.
  - Page 9, "The interview guide": our first five questions, not anyone's answers:
    1. When did you first decide something had to change?
    2. Who else had a say, and what did each need?
    3. What nearly stopped it?
    4. What did we say that you repeated to someone else?
    5. What would have made yes easier?

**Who it's for**
- Statement: The CRM says you lost on price. [N]
- The right project: You sell to committees and lose deals you expected to win. Two quarters of deals have closed, won and lost, and someone on your side will run the next round.
- The wrong project: A standing programme or a dashboard comes only with the system. If losses trace back to accounts you should never have chased, interviews will only confirm it.

**The scope**
- Claim: Buyers tell an outsider what they won't tell your rep. [N]
- In:
  - An agreed number of interviews from the last two quarters' closed deals
  - Deals chosen with you, with buyers who agree to talk
  - The write-up, in the buyers' own words
  - The interview guide, for your next round
  - A readout session with leadership
- Not in:
  - A standing win/loss programme
  - Surveys or NPS
  - A dashboard
  - Rewriting your messaging from the findings
  - A promise that every buyer will talk

**What it takes**

Everything hangs on buyers agreeing to talk. We choose the deals together and send the requests in week one; if
too few say yes by its end, the project stops there and the guide is yours. Leadership gives an hour for the
readout.

**Where it leads**
- In the system it becomes a standing part of every quarterly review, and its findings feed the objection answers in your Narrative OS.
- Link (.ac-go): The GTM Intelligence layer →

**Close**
- Line: Every lost deal has a buyer who knows why. (9 words)
