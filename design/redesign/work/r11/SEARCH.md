# Being found: search and AI answers

Round 11 · 19 October 2026 · the plan in plain words

In round 10 you asked: "I just want to confirm if we are missing any pages that might be very necessary for SEO
visibility, search engine visibility, as well as visibility on AI and stuff like that." The sitemap (SITEMAP.md,
beside this document) answers the part about pages. This document is the whole plan: what we do on the site, what we
do off it, how we'll know it works, and what we need from you. Our own list for building it is in SEARCH-BUILD.md. You
don't need to read that one.

**How far to trust it.** Some outside pages would not open from here. For those we read a search engine's summary,
and the sources at the end mark them "as reported". A few pages did open; they are marked "read at source". "Our
judgment" means our own reading, not a measurement. Section 8 lists what we still check before launch.

## 1 · The short answer

- **For Google, AI answers are search answers.** Google's guide to its AI features says optimizing for them "is still
  SEO": the same index and the same ranking, with no special files and no special code [1]. ChatGPT, Claude and
  Perplexity keep their own indexes and run their own search crawlers, and we let them in. So we build nothing only
  for AI. We do ordinary search work properly, and make every page easy to quote.
- **On the site, three things:**
  - pages with real substance: the fixed-scope engagements, the field notes, one honest comparison of kinds of help, a
    standards page, and a glossary soon after launch;
  - every page written so it can be quoted: a plain answer at the top, terms defined, facts in text and sources beside
    numbers;
  - the technical basics, none of which exist today. We do these; they need no decision from you.
- **Off the site:** one approved description of Kelwin, used word for word everywhere. Then mentions we earn, never
  ones we buy. In one study of about 75,000 brands, how often a brand was mentioned across the web was the strongest
  link to being named in Google's AI answers [2].
- **Closed on 19 October:** you made the project's files on GitHub private, so they no longer show your name, our
  prices or our working notes, and today's public preview is dark. Where the project lives next is DECISIONS.md,
  decision 13.
- **How we'll know:** visits from AI assistants, counted; "An AI assistant" as an answer on our forms; Google's and
  Bing's own reports; and a monthly check, by hand, of what five assistants say about Kelwin.
- **What we won't do:** location pages, doorway pages, pages about named rivals, "best agencies" lists, a price page
  without prices, a separate questions page, invented authors, bought links.
- **What we can't promise:** a ranking, or a date by which assistants will name Kelwin. We check every month what the
  assistants say about us, and correct the source when they're wrong.

## 2 · Why this matters for Kelwin

- **Buyers research with AI.** In Forrester's January 2026 survey, 94% of nearly 18,000 B2B buyers used AI in their
  latest purchase [3]. It is mostly about software, not services, so read it as a direction, not a forecast for Kelwin.
- **The buyers we want search for a problem or a service, not for a firm** (our judgment). That is why each
  fixed-scope engagement gets a page, and why most field notes answer one problem.
- **Assistants quote what is clear, sourced and near the top.** One study found ChatGPT takes 44.2% of its citations
  from the first 30% of a page [4].
- **A high Google ranking matters less than it did.** In Ahrefs's data, 38% of the pages Google's AI answers cited in
  March 2026 came from the top ten results, down from 76% in July 2025 [5]. Being the clearest source on a question
  counts.

### 3.2 · How every page is written

Writing rules for the stage when every page is written, before it is designed.

1. **A plain sentence under every headline:** what the page is, and who it is for. The headline can stay a statement
   in our voice; the sentence under it does the explaining.
2. **Every Kelwin term defined once, where it lives,** in the form "Revenue entropy is …". The glossary quotes these
   sentences word for word.
3. **Questions as headings** wherever a page answers questions: the audit's six, Engagements' questions about terms,
   and each fixed-scope engagement's answer block. That block has four questions: What is it? Who is it for? What's
   included? How is it priced, and how long does it take? The last is answered without numbers ("A fixed fee and a
   length, both set in the written scope. We give the starting price and the usual length on the first call."),
   because you decided in round 9 to keep prices and lengths off the site. No separate questions page: Google stopped
   showing question-and-answer results on 7 May 2026 [7].
4. **Every outside number with its source beside it,** in the text, not only in a footnote or a drawing.
5. **Comparisons as tables of text,** such as the comparison note.
6. **Every fact in a drawing, a film or an animation also written in text nearby:** the six-month ruler, "One quarter,
   step by step", the globe's "Global footprint". Machines don't read drawings reliably.
7. **Specimen numbers labeled "Specimen" in the same block of text,** and kept out of Google's search snippets with an
   instruction Google honors. That carries your rule against invented proof into AI answers.
8. **Real dates on field notes:** published, and "Updated" only when the content really changed. Assistants favor
   newer pages [8].
9. **Link words that say where they go** ("The email deliverability engagement", never "Learn more").
10. **Every word in the page as it is sent,** never added later by the browser. Many AI crawlers are reported not to
    run scripts (not checked). Rows that open in place keep their text in the page, hidden until opened.

Two things in today's build to fix: five headlines read as joined words to a machine ("fixingthe wrong layer"), though
a browser shows them correctly; and decorative captions near the top of a page are read as text straight after the
headline, so we keep them few.

### 3.3 · Letting the assistants in

**The AI companies send two kinds of reader.**
- **The search kind** looks pages up to answer a question: ChatGPT's, Claude's, Perplexity's, Apple's, and Google's and
  Bing's own. **We let all of them in.** Blocking one takes Kelwin out of that assistant's answers.
- **The training kind** collects pages to teach the next model. Google and OpenAI each say blocking theirs doesn't
  affect search [9]. Whether to let them read kelwin.co is DECISIONS.md, decision 9 (the sitemap's decision 3). We propose yes.

**What machines read, in plain words** (our list for building it is in SEARCH-BUILD.md):
- a list of every page we want found, with the date each one really changed;
- one file of rules for crawlers, which lets them in;
- a short fact sheet for assistants. Almost none read it yet and Google ignores it, but it takes about an hour and
  makes us write Kelwin's facts down once [10]. It is the first thing we drop if launch is tight;
- on every page, a short block naming the firm (Kelwin, New York, no founder, no headcount, no legal suffix) and what
  the page is. It helps some assistants and is never the only place a fact appears;
- a proper title and a one-line description on every page, written last from the approved copy. Today's still
  describe pages that no longer exist ("no CRM, no sequence", "the Early Access Protocol", "the founder's memo");
- a preview card for every page, made from its headline in type and flat color, so a LinkedIn share shows a proper
  card instead of a bare address;
- the feed of field notes;
- the file that tells researchers where to report a weakness.

### 3.4 · Moving to kelwin.co

- **Every address is chosen once, before launch.** Google follows a forward from an old address, but a forward made
  by the page itself is a weaker signal than one made by the server, and any forward takes time to be picked up [11].
  So we don't rename after launch.
- **Done on 19 October:** the project's files on GitHub became private, and today's preview went dark. Moving the
  project into a GitHub organization in Kelwin's name is DECISIONS.md, decision 13.
- **The new host** is that work's choice. Whatever it chooses, every address in the sitemap stays the same.
- **At launch:** the old preview addresses answer "not found". That costs nothing, because nothing public links to
  them. The new host's own spare address for the site is switched off or hidden from search, so there is never a
  second public copy of Kelwin. Its settings for AI crawlers are set to match decision 3.
- **On launch day we check** that every page answers at its kelwin.co address, that an unknown address answers "not
  found" (not "OK"), that the hidden pages stay hidden, and that Google's and Bing's reports are set up.

### 3.5 · Speed

Google counts a page as fast when its main content shows within 2.5 seconds, it answers a tap within 0.2 seconds, and
it barely jumps while loading [12]. Four risks on this site (our judgment, to measure):

1. **The page turn.** On the look work's films, the full-logo turn takes about 1.3 seconds from a click to the new
   page, and on arrival from outside it would cover the page for about 0.8 seconds. For search we recommend it plays
   only when you click between pages. When to play it is a look decision, asked with the films.
2. **Entrance animations** must never leave a headline invisible while it waits.
3. **The hero film and the globe** load after the headline.
4. **Pictures** carry their size, so the page doesn't jump.

## 5 · How we'll know it works

Nothing here needs a paid tool at launch.

1. **Can Google and Bing see every page?** Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, the free reports for site
   owners, set up on kelwin.co at launch, show which pages are listed and which searches they appear for.
2. **Do AI assistants send visitors?** The analytics counts visits from ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot
   and the other assistants as their own group. Visits from the assistants' apps often arrive with no source and count
   as direct (our judgment).
3. **Do buyers say so?** "How did you hear about us?" on the audit and the contact form gets the option "An AI
   assistant (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot)". It catches what the analytics miss, and is kept with each
   lead in the CRM.
4. **What do the assistants say?** Once a month, about an hour: the same 12 questions, asked in a fresh, signed-out
   session of ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google with AI Mode. For each answer we note three things: is
   Kelwin named? Is a kelwin.co page cited, and which? Are the facts right (the name, New York, the audit, no legal
   suffix, no named person, Scaling's status)? Questions about Kelwin by name are asked twice, because answers vary.
   - About Kelwin (are the facts right?):
     1. "What is Kelwin (kelwin.co)?"
     2. "Who is behind Kelwin, the go-to-market firm?"
     3. "How does Kelwin's System Audit work?"
     4. "What does Kelwin charge?" (The right answer: the starting price is given on the first call.)
   - What Kelwin is (are we found?):
     5. "Who designs and runs revenue systems for B2B companies, not as a lead-generation agency?"
     6. "Alternatives to a B2B lead-generation agency for a Series A company"
     7. "What does a GTM systems partner do, and how is it different from a lead-generation agency?"
     8. "What is revenue entropy?"
   - Problems buyers bring:
     9. "Our reply rates fell but every email shows delivered. What's wrong?"
     10. "How should we set up our CRM stages for a 10-person sales team?"
     11. "Fractional CRO, agency or in-house hire: which should we choose?"
     12. One question per fixed-scope engagement, in rotation, for example "Cold email setup for B2B: who does it
         properly?"
5. **Which pages bring audits?** Once a quarter, from the analytics and the CRM: the pages each audit request read
   first, and where the visit came from.

Paid tools that automate the monthly check exist (prices not checked). We leave them until the hand check shows
Kelwin being named.

**What good looks like.** These are our estimates, not promises:
- within 90 days of launch, all five assistants describe Kelwin correctly when asked by name;
- in the same period, no answer adds a legal suffix or names a person. The project's files are private now, so this
  holds as long as your own LinkedIn profile doesn't name you as behind Kelwin (section 7). If it does, an assistant
  may name you, and that is true;
- being cited for questions about what Kelwin is (question 7) takes longer, and depends on the notes, the Index and
  mentions elsewhere.

When an answer is wrong, we fix it at the source: the page, the profile or the facts. We never argue with the
assistant.
