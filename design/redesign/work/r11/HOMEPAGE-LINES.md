# The homepage lines, rewritten

Round 11 · 19 October 2026 · each line for your yes

In round 10 you said yes to rewriting seven homepage lines: "YES, REWRITE ALL OF THEM". You also wrote that the
voice "feels a little too much like an agency". This document gives each of the seven a new line, written to the
second version of the voice guide (`design/redesign/work/r11/VOICE.md`). It ends with the other homepage lines that
now clash with your round-10 answers or the new guide, each with a proposed line.

Nothing on the site has changed. Each line goes in only with your yes.

**A second read.** Two readers went through the first draft of these lines: one looking only for agency habits, and
one role-played as a buyer, the CEO of a $30M B2B company who has hired a strategy firm once and three agencies.
Both liked the letter in "we" with the agency gone, "More of the same" for the "no" row, and the move toward who
decides. They found the letter's turn, the section 07 headline and the hero line weak, and asked for one more pass
on the "yes" row, the method note and the final call. Those lines are rewritten here. The main changes:
- "Run it with your team" is gone. It is how outsourced sales and RevOps firms describe themselves ("an extension of
  your team"), and it had become a slogan. We say "we run it", and once on the page who decides.
- "Six assets hold the learning" is gone: an abstract noun doing the work, close to a slogan the round-10 guide had
  already retired ("Learning is the asset.").
- The letter's last line no longer says we take the first call "ourselves", which is a small firm's badge. It says
  where the founding team is: the readout and the decisions after it.
- The hero line no longer claims to be what "a B2B company's revenue depends on", which nobody can check.

**What every new line holds to**
- **Retainer first.** We build the system and we run it. What we build lives in the client's own accounts and
  documents. We hand it over only when a client asks, so no line is about us leaving.
- **Where the system lives is said once on the page,** in section 07 (other line 07b). The other new lines each say
  something different, so the page doesn't repeat itself.
- **No names and no "I".** The letter speaks as "we" and is signed by the founding team.
- **Less agency.** No agencies as the foil, no "in your name", no "free", no "with your team", no list of our tasks
  on your emails and decks.
- **What you liked stays:** plain words, a doer in every sentence, short sentences, no long dashes.
- **Each line keeps its place:** about the same length, the same line breaks in headlines, one light phrase per
  headline, and the same job in the story.

**The seven, read in order down the page** (the letter and "Who it's for" each have two of them)

| Section | The new line | Its job |
|---|---|---|
| 04 · The letter | "So we build a system that keeps that learning inside the company, and we run it." | The problem, and what we do about it |
| 07 · The six assets | "Six assets / your team uses." | What the system is made of, and who uses it |
| 08 · The method | "After the audit, we build the system and run it, on one operating retainer." | How it is bought |
| 09 · Who it's for | "A leader who decides" (yes) · "More of the same" (no) | Whom we take on, and whom we decline |

## 2 · The letter's turn: "installs the system, then hands you the keys"

**Today**

> So we built the firm we kept wishing existed — one that installs the system, then hands you the keys.

**New**

> So we build a system that keeps that learning inside the company, and we run it.

**Other ways to write it**
- "So we build the system and run it, and the learning stays inside the company." The same idea, with the system
  first.
- "So we build the system, write each quarter’s learning down, and run it." It answers "nobody writes the language
  down" directly, but "write down" is our task again.

**Why**
- "Hands you the keys" made handover the promise, and "keys" is a metaphor the guide retires.
- The first draft read "So we build the system, write the language down, and stay to run it with your team." Both
  readers caught it: "write the language down" sells our writing, "stay" answers a fear about leaving, and "with
  your team" is the outsourced team's line. The new line answers the paragraph's own problem (the learning leaves)
  with what the system does (keeps it in the company) and says we run it, without a partner clause.
- It stays in the movie's third person ("the company"), so the paragraph doesn't switch to "your team" halfway.
- "The firm we kept wishing existed" reads as a stock origin line for small firms, so it went too.
- "Build", not "install": the guide keeps Install as the name of a phase of the method and uses "build" in sentences.
- It still leads into the next section, which draws the system.

**How it fits.** 80 characters, against 101 today. The paragraph keeps its five lines.

## 3 · Section 07: "If we leave, the system stays."

**Today** (the second line is the light phrase)

> If we leave,
> **the system stays.**

**New**

> Six assets
> **your team uses.**

**Other ways to write it**
- "Six assets, / revised quarterly." It says we keep running the system, without saying that we stay. It doesn't name
  who revises them.
- "Each quarter / builds on the last." It answers the letter's "each quarter starts from zero", but the quarter
  becomes the doer, and the page already says "each quarter" often.

**Why**
- The headline is no longer about us leaving. The guide's rule 18 allows no headline, lede or closing line about
  that.
- It is concrete. It names what the section shows (the six assets) and who works from them every day (your team),
  and the sentence under it says the rest: the system lives in your own accounts and documents, and we run it from
  there. Your team uses it, and we run it: that is the split a partner offers.
- The first draft, "Six assets / hold the learning.", let an abstract noun do the work. It is close to "Learning is
  the asset.", a line the round-10 guide had already retired as a slogan.
- Both readers' first choices were too wide for the line: "your team works from." measures 897px and "revised each
  quarter." 838px at the real size, against about 700px of room.
- It isn't the round-10 idea for this line ("Everything we set up sits in your accounts and in your name, and we run
  it with you every month."). "In your name" reads as someone emailing as your company, and "every month" as account
  management.
- For the whole section to read retainer first, three more things in it change: the label, the sentence under the
  headline, and the drawing's "Handover" line (other lines 07a to 07c).

**How it fits.** Measured at the real size (88px): "Six assets" is 416px wide against 438px for "If we leave,", and
"your team uses." is 638px against 696px. The text beside the headline starts at about 755px, so it clears. Same two
lines, same light second line. On a phone (44px) the second line is 319px wide in a 358px column.

## 4 · The method (section 08): the note beside "It starts with the audit."

**Today**

> **Engagements** Install first, then an operating retainer — on a system you own.

**New**

> **Engagements** After the audit, we build the system and run it, on one operating retainer.

**Other ways to write it**
- "After the audit, the operating retainer starts with the build." The first draft. It makes the fee the subject of
  the sentence, and "the build" is studio slang.
- "After the audit, the operating retainer starts with a three-month pilot." This names the pilot. The guide keeps the
  pilot on the Engagements page, so use it only if you want the pilot on the homepage.

**Why**
- Today's note puts the retainer after the build, as an extra. In your model the retainer starts with the build: the
  build happens in the pilot, months 1 to 3 of the retainer. The new note says that in the right order, with us as
  the doer and the fee last.
- "One operating retainer" says the build and the running are one engagement, without putting contract terms on the
  homepage.
- "On a system you own" goes. Where the system lives is said once on the page, in section 07.
- "Operating retainer" is the homepage's approved name for the fee.
- It doesn't say what the retainer includes, so it leaves room for the setup fee you raised in round 10.
- Once the Engagements page exists, this note is a natural place for its link. The sitemap work settles that.

**How it fits.** 75 characters, against 64. Still two lines in the note's column (15px, 32 characters wide): "After
the audit, we build the system and / run it, on one operating retainer."

## 5 · Who it's for (section 09): the open "yes" row, F-02

**Today**

> **F-02 · Someone will own it** · An owner inside.
> After handover the system is yours — run by your operator, or with us on retainer.
> Owning it is the real gate.

**New**

> **F-02 · A leader who decides** · A sponsor at the top.
> Your CEO, or whoever owns the number, decides what changes each quarter.
> We answer to them, and run the system in between.

**Other ways to write it**
- "Your CEO, or whoever owns the number, joins each quarterly review." / "We answer to them, and run the system in
  between." This keeps attendance as the test, which reads as a meeting rhythm.
- Keep today's title and short line ("Someone will own it · An owner inside.") and change only the two sentences.
  This is the smallest change, but "it" in the title no longer points at anything clear.

**Why**
- The old test was whether you would own the system after handover. The test now is whether someone at the top on
  your side makes the decisions. A system with no senior sponsor stalls, so it is a true reason to say yes, and
  saying it out loud is how a partner chooses its clients.
- The first draft, "Pipeline has an owner", was a test almost every company passes (every head of sales owns
  pipeline), and it read like a RevOps firm's checklist. Its "joins each quarterly review" made a meeting the entry
  test. A sponsor who decides is a higher bar, and it is the one that matters.
- "Whoever owns the number" is the reader's own phrase for the revenue target. A partner's sponsor sits at the top;
  a supplier answers to whoever manages it.
- "We answer to them" shows close service through who we report to, with no adjective (voice guide, section 4).
- "Run the system in between" states retainer first as the normal case. "Operator" and "the real gate" go (the
  guide's never list has "the real" anything).
- The title changes with the sentences, because "Someone will own it" has nothing left to own. That goes a little
  past the line you said yes to, so the title needs your yes as well.
- This row is now the homepage's one line about who decides, so the final call no longer says it (row 7).

**How it fits.** Each sentence still sits on one line at full width: 605px and 403px, against 629px and 196px today,
in a row about 659px wide. The title is 20 characters against 19; the short line is 21 against 16, and both sit on
one line in the row head.
