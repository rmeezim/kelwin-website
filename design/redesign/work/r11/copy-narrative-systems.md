## What changed from the round-10 rewrite

You wrote, on this page: "Your approach this time is pretty much exactly what I was looking for, but still, now it
feels a little too much like an agency." And: "It sounds like every other agency now, kind of, to be honest."

The round-10 page had a doer in every sentence, but the doer was nearly always us, working on emails, decks and
inboxes. That is how an agency describes itself. This version keeps the plain sentences and changes what they are
about: the decision this part of the system holds (what each buyer hears from you), the asset that holds it
(Narrative OS), and the rule that keeps it honest (a line stays only while buyers' evidence supports it).

**A second read.** Two readers went through the first draft of this version: one looking only for agency habits,
and one role-played as a buyer, the CEO of a $30M B2B company who has hired a strategy firm once and three agencies.
Both found the agency feel mostly gone, and five habits left. This version fixes them:
- **The point of view was the stock one.** "Buyers' words first, including buyers who said no" is the stated method
  of most positioning and win/loss firms. The new point of view is one only a firm that runs the system each quarter
  can keep: each quarter we bring your leadership the lines the evidence no longer supports, favorites included.
  Buyers first stays, as the order of work in the first edition.
- **The section on what we see opened on a stock line.** "Ask three of your people what you sell" opens many
  positioning pitches. The heading is now our own reading: your champion makes your case in meetings you're not in.
  The test stays, as one sentence in the body.
- **One phrase became a tagline.** "Won and lost deals" appeared about ten times, and the page defined itself three
  times. Now the phrase sits in the headline and in the third stage, and Narrative OS is defined once, under the
  headline.
- **The stages still read as our process.** Read the notes, call buyers, write versions, test them, roll the winner
  out. Now each edition is the subject of its stage, and the stages say what each edition holds.
- **The buyer's own words were missing.** Positioning and messaging, the words a buyer searches for, now appear in
  the first sentence under the headline and in section 4.

Three pairs show the change from round 10.

**1 · The first screen**
- Before: "A pitch your buyers / can repeat without you." Then: "With you, we write down what you sell, who it's for
  and why buyers should pick you. We test the wording in your first emails, then rewrite your website, deck and call
  guides around the winner. After that, we keep testing new wording every month." Then a row of four facts, among
  them "Who sends the emails · We do, from inboxes in your company's name; your reps take the replies".
- After: "What each buyer hears from you, / built from your won and lost deals." Then: "Narrative OS is your
  positioning and messaging as one written record, with the proof behind each claim. We build it from what buyers
  said when they chose you or turned you down. A line stays only while that evidence supports it." The row of four
  facts is gone.
- Why: the old screen was a list of our tasks, and its first fact was the promise of an outbound
  appointment-setting firm. The new screen names a decision, the asset that holds it, in the buyer's own words, and
  the rule that keeps it current. Who sends, the meeting rhythm and the contract length move to the Engagements page
  and the proposal.

**2 · What the work produces**
- Before: "The work starts with four short documents. We keep them in one folder in your own drive, named Narrative
  OS." And the refusal: "We don't design logos, build websites or buy ads. We write the words, and your designer,
  developer or agency works from them."
- After: "Four parts, each settling a question your people answer differently." Then: "Each line of your
  positioning and messaging sits beside the buyer evidence behind it, so a new rep sees why." And the limit: "This
  work is for companies where several people sign off on each deal."
- Why: "four short documents in one folder" is what a freelancer hands over. The old refusal placed us in the
  client's line of suppliers, as the copy shop beside the designer and the developer. The new limit is about fit.

**3 · How it runs**
- Before: "Months 1 to 3 · the pilot / We write the pitch with you and test it.", with three rows under each stage:
  "What we do", "What you do" (for example "Your CEO and head of sales join our working session (every two weeks on
  Launch, weekly on Growth)") and "What you get".
- After: "Each edition starts from what your most recent buyers said." Three stages on one ruler, each an edition
  of Narrative OS: "The first edition, from your buyers" · "The second edition, from what held up" · "A new edition
  from the latest won and lost deals".
- Why: "what we do, what you do, what you get" under month headings is a subscription's "how it works". The stages
  are now the history of the asset, which keeps the hierarchy you asked for in round 9 and drops the statement of
  work.

**What stays from round 10:** the "Many voices, one pitch" drawing; the order of work (buyers first, leadership
second); the test a reader can run ("Ask your CEO, your head of sales and your newest rep..."), now in the body; the
Gartner figure with our reading kept apart from it; the specimen message matrix for the invented company, with four
answers struck out and a new sentence written by us; an honest answer for when the message isn't the problem; and
every craft rule (a doer in every claim, plain words, no long dashes, no metaphors, nothing said twice).

**Other headlines we considered**
- "One record of what you tell buyers, / kept true to your won and lost deals." Close, and "record" suits the asset.
  But "kept true" is vaguer than "built from".
- "What each buyer hears from you, / decided from your won and lost deals." The first draft. "Decided from" trips on

buyer hears), says what it rests on, and needs no other line to make sense. A CEO's restatement, by our own read:
"they build what our buyers hear from our won and lost deals" (11 words, naming the decision). The guide asks for
this restatement from someone who didn't write the page, so it still needs an editor's read.

## 5 · How it runs

> **Each quarter after**
> **A new edition from the latest won and lost deals**

> When a competitor changes its pitch, you ship a feature or new reps join, your messaging has to change too. Your

> How the buyer calls work →

> The System Audit checks that before any engagement begins, and its findings name what does hold them back. If a
