# Round 11 · Your decisions

Round 11 · 19 October 2026 · one list, for your answers

This round ran five pieces of work side by side: the offer, the map, the voice, the look, and what sits behind the
site. Each ended with its own list of decisions, and in places those lists disagreed. We have put them together into
one list of 16. Two of them, on the look, are filled in once its films are ready.

Each decision gives the question, the choices, our proposal and why, and where to read more. Your round-10 answers
(the sitemap, the three packages, the 65% pilot, the money terms, the day-90 exit, no prices on the site, the drawings,
today's page turn with the full logo, the voice, the order of work) stand and are not asked again.

After the list: four facts we need from you; what we settled ourselves; where the documents disagreed and how we
settled it; and where each earlier decision went.

## The offer

### 1 · What we call the two families

What should the smaller engagements, and Launch, Growth and Scaling, be called?

- **A. Fixed-scope engagements, and retained engagements** *(we propose)*
- B. Standalone engagements, and retained engagements
- C. Fixed-scope engagements, and your word: comprehensive engagements
- D. Your words: micro engagements, and comprehensive engagements

**Why.** "Fixed-scope" says how the work is bought, not how small it is, and it fits every one of them, dinners
included. "Retained" states the retainer. "Micro" makes the work sound small, and "comprehensive" claims size. With A,
the homepage's two tags on the audit change to "A ten-day diagnostic" and "10 working days · one readout", so "fixed
scope" names only the paid family. "Package" and "tier" never appear on the site.

*Where:* OFFER 2.3 · VOICE 2.4 and its decision 4 · HOMEPAGE-LINES, lines 01d and 10c.

### 2 · What goes into Launch, Growth and Scaling, and what they cost

Do we add to the packages, and reprice Growth?

Our proposal:
- Every package gains conference activation (one show a term on Launch, one a quarter on Growth), training the
  client's team on what we wrote, and its named accounts called what they are: account-based marketing.
- Launch also gains about six calls with recent buyers in month 1, so its first positioning starts from buyers' own
  words, as the Narrative Systems page promises. Growth and Scaling already include buyer interviews.
- Scaling's named-account play becomes about 10 accounts worked one to one, and the rest in clusters.
- A short add-on menu, at most three on any proposal, one price each: executive dinners (Growth and Scaling, once a
  producer is named), webinars, retargeting, more named-account depth, team training. Texts and WhatsApp only as
  reminders to people who said yes. Posts written for a client's founder are held.
- Launch stays at $8,000. Growth rises to $20,000. Scaling gets no price until it is costed seat by seat; the site
  describes it in full and marks it "not yet taking clients".

- **A. As proposed, with Growth at $20,000** *(we propose)*
- B. As proposed, with Growth at $17,500
- C. As proposed, with Growth staying at $15,000
- D. As proposed, but Scaling shown as "by arrangement"

**Why.** Costed seat by seat at middle contractor rates, Growth at $15,000 costs more to deliver than it earns. At
$20,000 it earns about what Launch does (31% against 37% once in its full run). The new inclusions cost about 4% of the
fee, and they are the "more" a buyer can feel. Scaling can't be staffed or priced yet, so the site says so plainly
while still showing all three.

*Where:* OFFER 1, 3 and 5.5 · SITEMAP decision 5 · Narrative Systems, "The number you were to set".

### 3 · The fixed-scope engagements at launch

Which smaller engagements do we offer at launch?

Our proposal: nine.
- Your six: email deliverability, positioning and messaging, the ICP and signal map, the CRM setup plan, win/loss
  interviews, and the operating model.
- The outbound build (our answer to cold email, for teams that will run it themselves), conference activation, and
  executive dinners on request once a producer is named.
- Each starts with a free scope check: up to three working days on the one piece, then a 30-minute readout.
- Held for later: the sales narrative, a plan for named accounts, a webinar series. Not sold on their own: cold

- A fourth question on the audit, about size (the sales team or revenue, never money), sends companies below Launch's
  size to these engagements instead of "not a fit yet".
- Fees from about $5,000 to $6,000 (deliverability) up to $25,000 (the operating model), each set in OFFER 4.1.

- **A. Nine, with the size question** *(we propose)*
- B. Nine, and the audit keeps its three questions
- C. Your six only at launch; the rest later

**Why.** Most of these prices sit mid-market and earn about 40–60% at middle rates, far more per hour than a pilot.
Each gets a page only where buyers search for it and we can deliver it today: 28 pages at launch, 30 with the outbound
build and conference activation.

### 4 · How a client starts a package, and what it can do at day 90

Your proposal to price the pilot by commitment is sound. This is our simpler version of it.

- Three ways to start, chosen on one call: six months fixed (pilot months at 55% of the fee), six months open at day
  90 (65%, the one we recommend), or month to month (the full fee plus 10%, with a one-off readiness fee).
- On both pilots, one fee of $250 per held first meeting, in months 1–3 only, capped so the pilot never costs more than
  three full months. Never "per lead".
- At day 90, the open option may leave for any reason. If we met every written target, it pays the readiness fee
  ($3,000 on Launch) instead of round 10's repayment of the discount. The fixed option leaves free only if we missed a
  target.
- In months 4–6, a client may leave without a fee if the written meetings target is missed two months running.
- Your fourth case (45% after the pilot) becomes this: at the day-90 readout, a client may sign for 12 months, and
  months 4–6 bill at the 12-month price, 10% off.

- **A. As proposed** *(we propose)*
- B. As proposed, plus a 70% "leave at any time" option that repays its discount if it leaves
- C. Your four options as you wrote them (65% and $300, 55% and $250, 70% and $350, 45% and $250)
- D. As proposed, but my fourth case meant something else (say which reading in OFFER 5.3: A, B or C)

**Why.** "Leave at any time" at 70% would cost less than month to month in every month, so nobody would choose month to
month. Three different meeting fees add a second dial worth about $800. The readiness fee makes every way out pay for
the build once.

*Where:* OFFER 5 and 7.6 · LIFECYCLE 3.8.

### 5 · The money terms: the revenue share, the deposit, month to month, renewal and the credit

- The revenue share: 3% of the first year's value of deals closed within 12 months of the held first meeting, as you
  proposed. Capped at 75% of the fees billed in months 1–6, taken together. No fixed ceilings. The same on every tier.
- No setup fee on six-month terms. A deposit of one pilot month instead of a full month ($8,800 at signing on Launch,
  fixed).
- Month to month at 10% above the fee: one number, not "5% to 10%".
- Renewal proposed in month 5. If nothing new is signed by month 6, month 7 runs at the same fee, then month to month.
  12 months at 10% off, 24 months at 15% off, no discount at six months.
- A fixed-scope engagement leads into a package: half its fee, up to one month of the package, comes off months 4–6 if
  the package is signed within 90 days of handover (you approved month 6 and 60 days).

- **A. All as proposed** *(we propose)*
- B. As proposed, but cap the share at 75% of each quarter's fees, as you wrote
- C. As proposed, plus 5% off a six-month renewal
- D. Keep round 10's terms wherever these differ

**Why.** Within six months, a cap per quarter pays the same or less, so one cap over the six months gives you more
upside, and it can never pass three quarters of what the client paid. A pilot-month deposit cuts the cash at signing by
a fifth to a third. A discount on a six-month renewal would undercut the first term.

*Where:* OFFER 6 and 7 · LIFECYCLE 3.9 and 4.4.

## The sales process

### 6 · Who leads, who is in the room, and who answers

You sell full time. Who does the rest?

- The GTM and revenue-architecture specialist leads each engagement as a contractor, committed for the client's whole
  term, with a named backup the client meets at kickoff. Proposals name every seat by role, marked founding team or
  contractor.
- The founding team is in the room at the first call, the readout, the proposal, the kickoff, day 90, the renewal
  talk, the last meeting and any bad news: about two hours a month per package.
- The site says it in one sentence, everywhere: "The founding team presents your readout, and is in the room at the
  kickoff, at day 90 and at each renewal." It never says the founding team runs every engagement.
- You take strong audit requests, with a phone alert and a reply within one working hour. One named contractor holds
  audit@ from 9 to 6, New York time, for everything else.
- The site promises a reply within one working day for the audit and for any message that names an engagement, and
  within two working days for anything else.

- **A. As proposed** *(we propose)*
- B. As proposed, but the site says only "We present every readout live, and we don't send it ahead."
- C. The founding team leads each engagement day to day (fewer clients at once)
- D. As proposed, but you answer every message yourself

**Why.** A buyer sold by senior people and then served by others turns on a firm by day 90, so we say exactly who is
where. Without a named owner for audit@, a strong lead waits behind your calls and the one-day promise breaks.

*Where:* OFFER 9 · LIFECYCLE 3.1 and 6 · VOICE, decision 1 · HOMEPAGE-LINES, the letter · BACKEND 4.5 and its

### 7 · Gifts

When do clients get gifts?

Our proposal: handwritten notes only until the client signs. Physical gifts only after signing, at most $100 a person
a year. Notes only for public-sector, healthcare and finance clients. Readouts sent as files, printed only on request.
Every gift logged. About $150–400 per six-month package.

- **A. As proposed** *(we propose)*
- B. Small gifts during the sale too, under $100, as you suggested
- C. Notes only, at every stage

**Why.** Buyers' gift rules and bribery laws target gifts given while a decision is open. After signing, a small gift
chosen for the person thanks them for work done, and fit matters more than price.

## Behind the site

### 12 · The services, and locking the accounts

Do we move the site to Cloudflare, set up the services, and lock every account?

Our proposal: Cloudflare for hosting, with our own small program for the forms; Resend for the instant replies;
HubSpot's free CRM; Buttondown for the email list; Cal.com for the first call; Plausible for visitor counts without
cookies; Google Workspace for mail. Two security keys for you, one password manager for the firm (Bitwarden Teams),
and a spare Google admin account kept offline. About $40–45 a month at launch, $85–145 by month 12, and $50–110 once
for the keys. Prices are last known around mid-2026 and are checked before anyone signs up.

- **A. Yes, as proposed** *(we propose)*
- B. Yes, but without the spare admin account
- C. Yes to the services; the keys and the password manager later

**Why.** GitHub Pages can't run forms, send security settings or keep drafts private, and its own terms say it isn't
for running a business. A stolen login is the most likely attack on a firm Kelwin's size, and a physical key is the
one defense a phishing page can't get past.

*Where:* BACKEND 1–3 and 8 · SECURITY 1 and 2.5.
