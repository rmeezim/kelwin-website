# Round 10 · The artifact language for the interior pages

What this is: the rules every drawing and animation on the interior pages follows from round 10 on, so the
inside of the site reaches the bar the approved homepage already sets. It answers the founder's round-9 notes:
the interior pages are "very bland, with almost minimal artifacts"; the site should "feel alive with artifacts
that keep grabbing the attention", with "a few wow moments about how we have engineered those artifacts to
actually convey the ideas", understandable "by even a 5-year-old or a 10-year-old" (ROUND9-NOTES.md, verbatim;
ROUND10-PLAN.md A17–A20, A24).

Studied for this: the six approved homepage drawings (hero, reframe, evidence, system, handover, method: their
markup, styles, scripts, notes and shots), the stitched homepage, the walk-through frames of every round-9
interior page (`site/walk8/`), the interior kit (kit §12–§13) and the round-9 plan's "AI-made" diagnosis.

---

## 1 · Why the homepage drawings read as engineered

**Each one draws a real object, and the object is the idea.** Three slabs stacked are "three layers, one
system". A ruler with phase bars is the engagement. Six documents crossing one line is ownership changing hands.
A warped lattice that snaps to a true grid over a cut block is "you're fixing the surface, not the
architecture". Nobody needs a legend: you recognise the thing, then you watch it work.

| Homepage drawing | The real thing drawn | What the motion shows | The solid part | How it plays | Settled |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hero (The Operating Feed) | a framed film and a measured chapter ruler | three takes, one per capability | the picture | frame plots, picture fades up, ruler ticks, chapter latches, hard cuts | ~2 s, then a take every 3 s |
| The reframe | a block cut into three bays under a lattice | the bent surface resolves into a true grid when you look one layer down | one red line | lattice plots, tags lift, a real 1.3 s morph, the cut draws, the red line seats | 4.5 s |
| Structural evidence | four published numbers and one measured bar | a number becomes a share of 100 | the numerals, a 10 px bar | numbers rise 120 ms apart, text writes in, the bar extends in 0.7 s | 2.6 s, then one reading every 3.3 s |
| The system | three slabs | layers go in one by one, close up into one system, the bottom feeds back to the top | the selected slab's red face | a slab every 1.25 s, close-up, the return line draws in 1 s, red goes home | 6.5 s |
| The handover | six documents and one line | dashed drafts cross the line and become solid pages; the old problem is struck, the new line rises | none: dashed against solid carries it | stack, fan out (1.1 s a sheet), strike and rise 01 to 06 | 4.3 s |
| The method | a phase track on a ruler | phases run once in order; the last one runs off the page | red audit bar, grey build bars, a white bar | ruler, ticks settle, bars extend phase by phase, the handover line, the bar runs off the page | 5.1 s |

What they share, looked at closely:

- **Drawing weights.** Almost everything is a 1 px line that stays 1 px at any size: faint for construction
  (often dashed), mid-grey for the object, white for the one that is selected. Against that, one solid part
  carries the subject: a red face, a bar, a set of numerals. The solid part is never decoration; it is the
  thing the sentence is about. Red appears once per view.
- **How labels sit.** Labels live outside the object and are tied to it: a leader from a 5 px square on the
  part, out to a short name in plain type. Measurements are real dimension lines with end ticks and a value
  ("1 system", "10 working days"). Text sitting over lines knocks them out, so nothing is hard to read. A
  label row sits above the drawing and a caption band below it, never inside the object.
- **Motion tells the mechanism in its own order.** Things happen in the order they happen in real life:
  layers are installed one at a time, then join; phases run left to right and the last one keeps running;
  drafts become solid at the moment they cross the line. The motion is the verb of the sentence.
- **Timing.** The heading lands first (under 0.8 s), then the drawing. Each move takes 0.5 to 1.1 s; a draw
  0.4 to 0.8 s; a fade 0.3 to 0.4 s. Between steps there is time to read (1.25 s on the system; 3.3 s per
  reading on the evidence). The whole pass settles in 4.5 to 6.5 s and plays once. Nothing bounces, nothing
  loops (one tiny 9.6 s square on the method is the only exception outside the film). Arrivals ease out; moves
  and morphs ease in and out.
- **The reader can take over.** Slabs, numbers and the surface/architecture switch are controls. Any touch
  stops the automatic pass for good.
- **The final frame explains everything.** With motion off, no script, or a screenshot, the drawing rests in
  its finished state and still tells the whole story.

## 2 · What the interior pages lack (from the round-9 walk-through)

1. **The picture under the headline doesn't move in steps.** GTM Intelligence's quarter is drawn complete and
   the frame simply rises and widens: it "gets to size and stays there", as the founder put it. GTM
   Infrastructure's picture is a document (the rules sheet), which reads as more text.
2. **After the head, the pages are type.** Service lists, runbooks, ledgers and two large statements.
   Infrastructure's "Order of work" is four short text blocks and a statistic; "Standing checks" is a
   three-row runbook. The founder: "a whole bunch of text slapped into one place".
3. **Round 9's cure went too far.** To stop drawings looking machine-made (funnels, knots, rings, boxed
   codes), round 9 allowed one object, one solid part and three labels. The clutter went, and so did the
   mechanism: the drawings now show that something exists, never how it works.
4. **Nothing can be touched.** Every homepage drawing is also a control; no interior drawing is.
5. **Every page moves the same way** (headline writes in, picture rises, sections fade up), so no page has a
   moment of its own.
6. **Contact says nothing about place.** A form and three steps; nothing about where or how the firm works.

The fix is not more decoration. It is drawing the real things we build and letting the reader watch them work.

## 3 · The rules (every interior artifact follows all twelve)

1. **One real mechanism, drawn as the real thing.** Each artifact shows one thing that really happens in our
   work, using the objects involved: a domain, an inbox, a CRM record, a calendar quarter, an email, a written
   rule. Never a metaphor (no funnels, knots, rings, lightning bolts, brains, rockets).
   *Test: you can say "this is ___ doing ___" in one sentence.*
2. **It plays as a process, in the order it happens.** Three to six steps, each a visible change of state,
   started one after another. Words that appear stay on screen at least a second before the next step. The
   pass settles in 4 to 7 seconds and plays once.
   *Test: frames taken every half second read like a storyboard.*
3. **Hairline drawing, one solid subject.** 1 px lines for structure and objects; a solid part for the thing
   that matters or moves (a bar, a block, a face, a filled record); red for at most one thing per screen.
   *Test: squint at the frame; the solid part is where your eye goes, and it is the subject.*
4. **Parts are named in plain words, on the drawing.** Up to six short labels (Mona, 14–15 px, sentence case)
   tied by a leader to the part they name. The code font is for numbers, dates and units only. No boxed codes,
   no letter prefixes, no "Fig.".
   *Test: no label needs another label to explain it.*
5. **A ten-year-old can follow it.** One plain sentence beside the artifact carries the idea; the artifact
   never needs a paragraph. Everyday words first; a trade term (SPF, CRM) only beside its plain meaning.
   *Test: cover the text; the motion still tells the story.*
6. **The finished frame is the explanation.** The markup is the end state. With reduced motion, without
   scripts, or in a screenshot, the whole mechanism is there, finished and readable. Motion only reveals it in
   order.
7. **Honest labels, said once.** Anything that is not a real Kelwin fact is marked once, in the label row:
   "Illustrative" (example numbers or settings), "Specimen" (an example document or company), "Worked example".
   Real figures carry their source. Never an invented client, office, person, team size or result.
8. **One wow per page, at most.** The wow is the moment the mechanism pays off (the voices snap onto one line,
   the quarter gathers into its readout, the event is routed). It goes to the page's main idea. Every other
   artifact on the page is calm.
9. **It is redrawn for the phone, not shrunk.** Each artifact has its own tablet and phone composition. Text on
   the drawing is at least 14 px at the size it is seen; nothing is wider than the screen; steps stack
   downwards. Checked at 1440, 834, 390 and 320.
10. **The reader can take over where it helps.** If there is a choice to explore (a step, an event, a layer),
    the artifact is also the control. Any press, tap or keyboard focus stops the automatic pass for good. A
    quiet "Replay" restarts it. Keyboard and screen readers reach everything.
11. **Kelwin's drafting language only.** Rulers, dimension lines with a number and a unit, the short red rule,
    registration marks where something is measured or registered, paper only for paper documents. No app
    windows, dashboards, glow, gradients, icons, 3D, bounce, parallax or scroll-jacking.
12. **Each artifact waits for its reader.** It starts when about 40% of it is on screen (a head picture: when
    the headline's last letter lands), never plays twice on scroll, and two artifacts never play in the same
    view. GSAP only where a timeline genuinely needs it.

## 4 · Where artifacts go (the page budget)

- Every interior page that explains a way of working gets: the picture under the headline, moving in steps;
  one to four body artifacts, each in its own section and each with one mechanism; at most one wow.
- Pages that are reading or legal text (notes, Privacy, Terms) keep type only. Artifacts are not wallpaper.
- Round 10 starts with five, each specified for its builder:

| Key | Page | The artifact | Its wow |
|---|---|---|---|
| infra | GTM Infrastructure | "How we build it": four small sections (the sending setup, data and signals, the routing rules, the CRM record), each with its own working drawing | the routing rules: an event arrives, a written rule fires, the next action goes out |
| intel | GTM Intelligence | (a) one quarter laid down week by week, gathered into the week-13 readout and fed into the next quarter; (b) "what happens next quarter" in three plain steps with one worked example | (a) the gather into the readout |
| narr | Narrative Systems | one company, five voices drifting apart; the written source appears; every voice snaps onto one line | the snap onto the line |
| map | Contact | a drafted world map: the working day as it moves round the world, New York as the registered office, the visitor placed live with the time difference measured | the visitor appearing, measured against New York |
| turn | every page change | two Kelwin-own alternatives to the Palantir-like turn: "On the line" and "The register" | (chrome, not a page wow) |

## 5 · For the builders (the numbers)

- **Strokes:** 1 px, non-scaling. Construction `--line` (dashed 2/4 where it is a guide), objects `--fg-2`,
  selected `--fg`, the one red line `--signal` at 1.5 px. Kit classes `.ac-s-faint .ac-s .ac-s-fg .ac-s-red`.
- **Solid parts:** `.ac-mass` (bars 6–13 px, faces, blocks; together at least 8% of the drawing);
  `.ac-mass--red` at most once per view. Nodes: 5–8 px squares (`.ac-node`, `.is-on` when lit).
- **Labels:** on-drawing 14 px Mona (`.ac-t-label`, or `.ac-callout--plain` in HTML), keys 15 px
  (`.ac-t-key`), `--fg-2`, the current one `--fg`; knock out lines behind text (`.ac-t-knock`). Numbers in
  JetBrains Mono 12 px (`.ac-numeral`). At most six labels visible in any one state.
- **Timing:** a draw 400–800 ms; a move 500–1100 ms; a fade 300–420 ms; between steps 600–1300 ms (≥ 1 s when
  the step shows words); a whole pass 4–7 s. `--ease-out` for arrivals, `--ease-inout` for moves and morphs.
  No overshoot. Hard cuts only in film.
- **Start:** an IntersectionObserver per artifact at 0.4 (not the slot: a fragment holding several artifacts
  starts each on its own); a head picture starts at `--ac-write-end` + 60 ms. Standalone builds (no
  `html.km-home`) play every artifact at once, staggered 600 ms, so the mk shot shows the finished state.
- **Behaviour:** `window.KM = { init(root, { reduced }) { …; return cleanup } }`. Timers and listeners in one
  list the cleanup clears. Reduced motion: no timers, final state, controls still work. Replay: reset with
  `.ac-cold`, then run from step 1.
- **Phone:** three geometries (1440, ~760, 358 wide), chosen by CSS with visibility (never `display: none` on
  anything that animates); the script animates only the visible one. Guard phone rules with
  `:root:not(.km-home)` (kit 12.13).
- **Access:** each artifact is a `<figure>` whose caption states the mechanism in one sentence; controls are
  buttons with `aria-pressed`/`aria-expanded`; `aria-live="polite"` speaks only after a reader's own action.

**Kit changes this implies (for the lead, after approval; nothing is edited now):** 12.8's "at most three
labels" becomes "at most six, each naming a real part"; registration marks are also allowed on measured
drawings (a map frame, a quarter); 12.12's "at most two play-once moments per page" becomes "one per artifact
section, one wow per page"; a synced step list (the stepper used by Intelligence (b) and Narrative Systems)
moves into the kit once both pages use it.
