## 2 · What it is

**What changes.** Round 9 never said what a Narrative System is. The first version of this rewrite then defined
it three times and spent a paragraph on why it is called a system. This version says it once, gives the reason
it matters, and states the choice we make that another firm might not.

**Label**

> What it is

**Heading**

> Everyone who sells for you works from the same written pitch.

**First paragraph**

> Ask your CEO, your head of sales and your newest rep what your company does, and compare the answers. Each of
> them talks to buyers, so the differences reach buyers too.

**Second paragraph** (one sourced number)

> Gartner (2019) found B2B buyers spend about 17% of their buying time meeting suppliers, all suppliers combined.
> Most of the rest goes on research and on talking among themselves. In that time, what they know about you comes
> from what you've published and what your reps have said, and from competitors and peers.

*Source pointer in the margin: Gartner, "The B2B buying journey", 2019, linked as on the 17% field note. "Most of
the rest" rests on the same research's split of the other 83% (from memory, about 45% researching alone and 22%
meeting within the buying group). We couldn't open the source from here, so check the split against it before the
page goes live, and cut the sentence if it doesn't hold. The last sentence is our reading, not Gartner's, and it
sits apart from the source on purpose.*

**Third paragraph** (our point of view)

> So we write the pitch from your buyers' words first, including buyers who said no, and from your leadership's
> words second. A buyer who said no is often the plainest about what they didn't understand.

*This is the page's point of view (voice guide, rule 23): a choice about how we work that another firm might not
make. It needs your yes (decision 7).*

## 4 · How it runs

**What changes.** Round 9 had two big statements of the same size ("We write it once, with your leadership in
the room." and "Then the person who runs it keeps it current."), side notes in a different order under each,
and "Your operator edits it" (handover first). Now there is one heading, one sentence under it, and three stages
on one timeline. Each stage has a time, a short heading and the same three rows. Buyers are now heard in month 1,
before anything is written. In the first version they were first heard in month 7.

**Label**

> How it runs

**Heading**

> We work in three stages, starting with a three-month pilot.

**The sentence under it**

> We work on a retainer, a monthly fee for ongoing work, for at least six months.

*If the pilot stays off the site (decision 2), the heading becomes "We work in three stages, and we test before
we rewrite anything." and the sentence under it is left out.*

**Stage 1**

> **Months 1 to 3 · the pilot**
> **We write the pitch with you and test it.**

> **What we do** · Before we write anything, we read your lost-deal notes and call recent buyers, won and lost, to
> hear how they describe you. Then we write the four documents with you and draft two or three versions of the
> message. We split new accounts between the versions in the first emails we send for you and in your reps' first calls. We count which
> version brings more replies and second meetings from the companies you want. We test before we touch your
> website or deck, so nothing is rewritten twice.

> **What you do** · Your CEO and head of sales join our working session (every two weeks on Launch, weekly on
> Growth) to review the test results and agree the next draft. We also need read-only access to your CRM, and notes or recordings from
> recent sales calls.

> **What you get** · The documents in your drive, a short note after each session, and a written comparison of
> the versions at the end of month 3.

**Stage 2**

> **Months 4 to 6**
> **We put the winning version where buyers see it.**

> **What we do** · We rewrite what buyers see around the version that won, and train your reps on the new call
> guides in one session. From month 4, each month, we test one new line in your first emails against the current
> one, and keep the winner.

> **What you do** · Your marketing lead signs off each page, slide and email before it goes live, and your reps
> join the training.

> **What you get** · A playbook for each channel you use, and a written list of what we changed and why.

**Stage 3**

> **From month 7, if you renew**
> **We keep the documents current.**

> When a competitor rewrites its pitch, you ship a feature or new reps join, the documents have to change too.

> **What we do** · Each quarter we read the latest calls with buyers who said yes or no. Wherever they describe you
> differently from how the documents do, we rewrite those lines and update what buyers see to match.

> **What you do** · Your CEO and head of sales agree the changes with us at the quarterly review, which takes about
> two hours.

> **What you get** · A new edition of the documents, with a list of what changed and why.

> **Where do the documents live?**

## What changed after the second read

**The main changes**
- **Each thing is said once or twice, not six or seven times.** The list of what we rewrite appeared six times; it
  now appears in the head and in section 3. "Why we call it a system" and its three parts are gone. The four head
  answers that repeated the sentence above them became four facts the first screen otherwise lacked.
- **The method holds up.** Buyers are heard in month 1, before anything is written. The specimen's winning
  sentence is new, not the company founder's. A version test is described as it would happen (we split new
  accounts between versions), and the winner is picked by replies and meetings from the companies you want,
  not by replies alone.
- **The facts a buyer asks about are on the page.** Who sends the emails (we do, from inboxes in your company's name; your reps take the replies). What happens
  monthly (one new line tested) and quarterly (the documents rewritten from buyer calls). What a retainer is.
  What happens if the pilot shows the message isn't the problem (for your yes).
- **Claims are at their true size.** "Every page, slide, email and script your buyers see" claimed more than we
  rewrite. "We can't show a client's documents" hinted at clients we can't show yet. "Yours from the first draft"
  promised contract terms nobody has set. All three are gone.
- **Plainer words.** "Sales scripts" became "call guides", which suits an enterprise buyer better. "Working
  session", "walk them through" and "the places buyers meet you" are gone. The audit has one name, the System
  Audit.

**Where we kept the first version, and why**
1. **The retainer's plain meaning.** The second read suggested "a fixed fee each month". During the pilot the fee isn't
   fixed (65% of the retainer, plus a fee per held meeting, and a revenue share), so the page says "a
   monthly fee for ongoing work".
2. **The row under the headline.** The second read asked both to replace the four answers and to drop the row. We kept
   a row of four, as short facts that answer what the first screen didn't (what you give, where it lives, how
   long, who sends). "What we don't do", which the second read proposed for the row, stays in section 3, where it
   already is.
3. **"Give the list once."** The head needs the list in summary and section 3 needs it in detail, with who does
   what. The voice guide now allows two places, as long as the second adds something.
4. **The Gartner sentence.** The second read's version ("Your website, deck and reps speak for you in that time") uses
   a metaphor and puts reps in conversations they aren't part of. Ours says where buyers' knowledge comes from,
   and names competitors and peers as the second read asked.
5. **"Each quarter something changes."** That is a general claim we can't show. The stage now opens with the
   events that make the documents change: a competitor's new pitch, a feature, new reps.
6. **The section 4 heading.** The second read proposed "We write your pitch with you, then test and fix it every
   month." It is now that section's ten-year-old sentence rather than its heading, because "every month" already
   appears twice on the page and the heading's job is to show the three stages.
7. **The working session.** The second read took it as padding. We kept it, and the copy now says what each
   session is for (the latest test results and the next draft). It follows each package's rhythm (decision 4).
