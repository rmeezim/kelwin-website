# Next steps: locking in the website

Round 10 · for your yes

## The short answer

You said we're going off all over the place, and you're right. We kept redesigning pages before the things they
depend on were settled: what we sell, which pages exist, what each page is for, and how we sound. So every round
reopened the last one.

From here, we lock the foundations once, in order, and only then design and build. Each stage ends with one
review from you, and nothing behind it reopens unless you change a decision.

## The order

### Stage 1 · Lock the offer (this round)
- Decide the business model: the three packages, the pilot, the levers, the exit, the project fees and how much
  the site says about price.
- We turn your answers into a one-page **offer sheet**: what we sell, to whom, on what terms. Every page and every
  first call quotes it, so the site, the proposal and the contract say the same thing.

### Stage 2 · Lock the map (this round)
- Approve the sitemap: 26 pages at launch, one of them new (Engagements), plus the pages that wait until they are
  real.
- Every page has one job, one audience and one door. A page that can't say why it exists doesn't get built.

### Stage 3 · Lock the message and the voice (this round, then one short follow-up)
- Approve the voice guide. Its sample is the Narrative Systems page, rewritten.
- We then write a one-page **message sheet**:
  - who we serve;
  - the problem in their words;
  - what we build;
  - how it runs on a retainer;
  - why us;
  - the proof we can honestly show.
  Every page's words come from it, so the pages stop contradicting each other.

### Stage 4 · Lock the look (this round)
- Approve the drawing rules and the three drawings, and choose the page turn.
- We fold them into the shared design kit, so every page gets them the same way.

### Stage 5 · Write every page before designing it (next round)
- Every page in the sitemap gets its words written out in full, like Narrative Systems: the sections, the exact
  words, and the drawing each section needs.
- We draft each page, then check it in a separate read against the voice guide's checklist.
- You review all the copy at once, in one sitting, and approve the words before any page is designed. This is the
  step we skipped before. Most of the "AI-made" and "vague" feedback was about words, and we kept fixing it in
  design.

### Stage 6 · Design and build, page group by page group
- Mockups follow the approved words and the drawing rules, in the sitemap's order:
  1. the homepage's new lines;
  2. Engagements;
  3. the three capability pages;
  4. Projects and the six project pages;
  5. Contact and the audit;
  6. Careers;
  7. Reports;
  8. About, Who We Work With and The Lab;
  9. Insights;
  10. legal, the menu and the footer.
- Your reviews at this stage are about how pages look and move, not what they say, because the words are already
  yours.
- Each approved group goes straight into the real site's code, so there's no second port at the end.

### Stage 7 · What has to be behind the site (in parallel with stages 5–6)
Today every form only opens the visitor's mail app, and the site keeps nothing. That can't take a CV or keep the
address of someone who asks for a report. Before launch we need:
1. **kelwin.co** pointed at the site, with audit@ and careers@ live and read every working day, and the domain's
   email settings passing the inbox checks. We sell exactly this, so our own setup must pass.
2. **A form service** that accepts file uploads (CVs) and sends each form to the right inbox and the CRM, for
   example Formspree or Basin.
3. **A light CRM and an email list** for our own pipeline, with tags, double opt-in and one-click unsubscribe, for
   example HubSpot's free tier or Attio, plus Buttondown or Kit.
4. **Cookie-free analytics**, so no cookie banner is needed, for example Plausible.
5. **A calendar** for the first call, soon after launch, shown only after the three questions qualify a visitor.
6. **Privacy and Terms** rewritten for all of the above, and read by a lawyer, going live the same day.

We set these up with you. You only need to create the accounts that must be in Kelwin's name.

### Stage 8 · Proof (starts with your first pilot)
Reports is the page that most shapes how we're seen, and the only page we can't fill by writing. Three steps:
- **Every client contract gets a publication clause** (anonymized by default, named with consent). That makes a
  report possible.
- **On day 1 of every pilot we record the baseline** (meetings, pipeline, reply rates), so the report at month 3–6
  has real before-and-after numbers.
- **With your yes, we ask the companies behind your past audits** whether we can quote what the audit found,
  anonymized, in a field note. Reports stays for client case studies with results.

Until then, Reports launches honestly: the report format, and "send me the first report".

### Stage 9 · Footage and photographs (any time before launch)
The approved pictures rule needs real shots. The shot list is ready:
- the Narrative Systems shot;
- About's portrait card and working session (named shots, no faces of people who haven't agreed);
- Who We Work With's real shot.

One half-day shoot covers them. Until then the pages carry their labeled stand-ins.

### Stage 10 · Launch
- Search basics: titles and descriptions, sitemap.xml, link previews, redirects from today's addresses.
- An accessibility check, a speed check, and analytics on the doors (audit started and finished, contact sent,
  report requested, application sent).
- Your go-ahead. Your round-7 condition stands: every page is redesigned before anything goes live.

### After launch
- One field note a month, so the list gets something real.
- Kelwin's LinkedIn company page.
- Once a quarter, a look at the numbers: which pages bring audits, and which don't.

## How we'll work from here
- **One review per stage.** Each has a fixed scope, and your decisions are recorded once (the notes files in the
  design folder).
- **We don't reopen a page unless one of your decisions changes.** If something needs a decision, it waits for
  the next review instead of being guessed.
- **Every review page leads with what matters most.** It asks only what we can't decide ourselves, each with a
  proposed answer.
