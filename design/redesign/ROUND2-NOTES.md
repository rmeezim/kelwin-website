# Round 2 — founder's picks and notes (verbatim)

General: "Throughout my selection, I have noticed that I was leaning a lot towards a creation of a blended design
language of both A and C in a lot of places, and sometimes A and B. If you could do that, that would be amazing.
Also, reduce the use of the logo symbol at every other place. It really looks tacky and a bit cheap and less
thoughtful. Use other kinds of shapes, [motions], and morphisms, whatever that is called."
(Speech-to-text: "Hey" = "A"; "emotions" = "motions"; "morphisms" = shape-morphing animation.)

Colour & type: Swiss Systems

01 Navigation & mega menu: A (The hanging plate) — "I like this hanging plate style of navigation and mega menu style. However, I feel like the use of the logo symbol across this navigation bar is a bit too much. Maybe we could do some other system drawing set kind of thing going on and make it a combination of the capability sheet style of navigation mega menu and the hanging plate. Combine them to make them a mixture of both, if that makes sense, because I don't like both those directions."
02 Hero: A (FIG. 01 · The Room) — "I really like this direction. To match it a bit with the design language of Plurel and make it feel like a family of specialized divisions of services, I feel like the statement saying 'Revenue is an architectural problem' should appear in two lines rather than three lines. To make it look a bit more proprietary, we could move the subtext, the main primary CTA, and the secondary CTA buttons towards the bottom-right area of the hero section, where the text saying 'A Fixed Scope Diagnostic' is located. Move that 'A Fixed Scope Diagnostic' text from the bottom-right corner into the bottom-left corner of the hero section. Almost vertically center-align the main heading text saying 'Revenue Is an Architectural Problem' while keeping it left-aligned. It's just an idea. Mix some of the design language with the observed room design language [hero C], if possible, or if there is room for it. Just a little bit, though, not too much. You are free to create a minor redesign of these sections that I'm giving my opinions on, so I can visually see what my thinking really looks like when put into practice. If you have any other better ideas, you're free to give them to me."
03 The reframe: A (Surface, then architecture) — "I really love the design direction of A, but I also love the design direction of C. The issue with design direction C in some sections is that it feels a bit too cluttered, and too much text is happening. Some text is way too small to read. If we could mix the design language of C with the design language of A to create something even more good-looking (where there is proper white space and restraint, while also feeling like the proper high-tech feeling that this direction C has), it would be a perfect blend of both."
04 Structural evidence: A (Four Into One) — "The only critique I have is that the animation thing, the box thing in the center, seems to have matched too much with the logo symbol. I want it to be a bit different because a lot of logo things are happening everywhere. It's a bit too repetitive and unoriginal feeling at this point, and it kind of cheapens the feel of the website. Make it some other kind of animation going on, but the rest of the section is amazing."
05 The stack (four layers): C (SHEET 04 · Stepped assembly) — "Create a blend of A, B, and C directions. Keep the proprietary art feel on the left side of the section, where there are layers of things animating. I love that, but make them more easily readable and understandable, and minimize and reduce the use of this logo symbol everywhere. Use different shapes, different designs for the same kind of animation."
06 System architecture diagram: A (The mark as circuit) — "I love the clean feeling of the A design direction. Meanwhile, the B and C also look equally as good. If we can incorporate the high-tech and sophisticated feeling of B and C into A by maybe incorporating that chart thing that B has and that 'How it closes' section that B has somewhere in A, and then use some of the C elements in A as well, it would create a blended design language."
07 State change: A (The mark, compounding) — "Create a combination of A and C design directions."
08 Operating principle band: C (Signal / noise sheet) — no note.
09 Methodology: A (The Phase Climb) — "Direction A seems very clean and minimal. Direction C is equally good-looking and more sophisticated-looking, yet it looks a bit too cluttered. In terms of text, especially, there's too much text going on. There's no easily understandable way of reading this, or a way to start and a way to not start reading this section. Create a blended approach of A and C where you keep the chart animation thing happening in C, meanwhile keeping the minimalism of A, and somehow incorporate them into a singular design."
10 Who it's for: B (Two Ledgers, One Gate) — "Improve the readability of some of the headings, like 'Built for' and 'Not for'. The rest looks pretty good. Combine some of the design language with the design direction of option C."
11 What you own: A (FIG. 08 · Handover kit) — "A combination of design direction A and design direction C, while again reducing the use of the logo symbol. It looks too tacky. B, too, because it's being used everywhere. Use more proprietary, different kinds of shapes and morphisms of different things happening when scrolling or automatically happening, whatever seems best for each section."
12 Before / after: A (Two-Plate Operating State) — no note.
13 FAQ: A (The core, answered) — no note.
14 Final call to action: A (The Core, Installed) — "I really like the design direction of A, but separate the CTA button from the '10 working days' thing into two separate things with proper visual hierarchy. Combine some of the design language with the design language of B to create a blended design language."
15 Footer: A (FIG. 11 · The mark, installed) — "I really like this design language, but remove that red dot thing in the logo symbol. Have the logo symbol buried a bit into the [bottom of the footer], half cut out — kind of too low and then half visible vertically and half not."
16 Interior page header: A (FIG. 01 film plate) — "I do not like any of the three options. Redesign the interior page headers completely from scratch to look more proprietary and more thoughtfully designed. I do have a little bit of bias towards C, but again, it doesn't look good. It looks too cluttered, and it looks too similar to the hero section on the homepage. I definitely do not like the B one."
