# Round 11 — plan (from the founder's round-10 answers; his words: ROUND10-NOTES.md)

Round 10 asked the founder to lock four foundations (the offer, the map, the voice, the look) and the order of work.
He approved the order and most of each foundation, with changes. Round 11 closes stages 1–4 on his changes, and
prepares stage 7 (what has to be behind the site), so stage 5 (every page written before it is designed) can start
on settled ground.

## A · Decided by the founder (binding)

### The map
- **B1 Sitemap approved, with changes.**
  - Check that no page needed for search visibility, or for visibility in AI answers, is missing.
  - **Two families of engagement.**
    - The comprehensive engagements are the three packages, Launch, Growth and Scaling.
    - The smaller ones, which he calls "micro engagements" ("or we could give it a more specific name"), replace
      "Projects": small, individual pieces of work for what a client needs at the moment, priced lower, as the way
      in and the upsell path into the packages.
    - Add more of them, for example email deliverability, cold email, cold calling, conference activation and
      executive dinners. Each may stand alone or be folded into a package, as we recommend.
  - **The Lab** teases the platform Kelwin is building: a comprehensive GTM/revenue platform that sits a level above
    the existing tech stack. Tease, don't give the idea away. A waitlist sign-up; the page is updated as the idea
    firms up.
  - **Insights** is the blog; field notes are its posts (our own research and points of view).
  - **Reports** stay as built: the client case studies.
- **B2 The site stores what visitors send: yes, set it up** (a form service, the CRM and the list).

### The offer
- **B3 The three packages: approved.** But he asks whether they give too little for the price next to peers such as
  Belkins, which bundle more (executive dinners, conference activation, webinars, SMS, WhatsApp, paid ads). He wants:
  - an answer on account-based marketing (is Scaling's named-account play ABM?), events and other in-person
    relationship work, and sales training or enablement for the client's team in the top tiers;
  - our proposal, tier by tier, of what is included and what is an add-on;
  - price perception kept, without pricing out buyers.
- **B4 The pilot fee: 65% recommended, accepted.** He proposes pricing the pilot by commitment, and asks what we
  think:
  - a day-90 exit option: 65% of the retainer and $300 per lead in months 1–3;
  - the full six months committed, no exit: 55% and $250 per lead;
  - leave at any time: 70% and $350 per lead;
  - a fourth case, "45%… $250 per lead… a full commitment" (its meaning needs settling: see the offer work).
- **B5 The other money terms: approved, with additions.**
  - Month to month, without the six-month commitment: 5–10% above the base retainer.
  - **Revenue share.** Keep 3%, but cap it at 75% of each quarter's fees (or of six months' fees), and count only
    deals closed within 12 months of the lead being sourced. Launch on these terms; Growth and Scaling may carry
    better terms for Kelwin if we advise it. The aim is fair upside on both sides.
  - A setup (infrastructure readiness) fee, since tools and infrastructure sit outside the retainer, or a
    smaller deposit to make starting easier.
  - Renewal proposed in month 5. Without a new term at month 6, it runs month to month at the 5–10% premium,
    unless the client signs for 6–12 more months, which earns a small discount.
- **B6 The day-90 exit is offered.**
- **B7 Micro-engagement fees: structure approved.** Research whether the prices are right, and whether more of them
  are needed for clients who don't need a package.
- **B8 Prices on the site: none.** The starting price is given on the first call.
- **B9 The lifecycle: redo it.**
  - One thorough, premium sales process for both families, without differentiating so hard that it hurts.
  - The customer experience comes first.
  - Small gifts (under about $100) during the sale, the engagement and the close.
  - Research how high-touch, premium engagements are run.

### The voice
- **B12 The voice: approved, but it still reads like an agency.**
  - Find the middle ground: a proprietary GTM, growth and revenue-operations partner, heavily focused on customer
    experience, very premium, proprietary and high-touch.
  - The design language has to say it too.
  - The Narrative Systems rewrite is redone on this.
  - The homepage lines: rewrite all of them (each comes back for his yes).

### The order of work and the company
- **B13 The order is approved, plus three items:**
  - automations that make the website a full inbound lead-generation machine;
  - a proper blogging system, so field notes are easy to post and to share across platforms;
  - full security against attacks.
- **B14 Delivery.**

  - The founder works full time (12–15 hours a day) on client acquisition and sales calls.
