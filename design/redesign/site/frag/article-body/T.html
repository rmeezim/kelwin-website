Most GTM operations are built as if selling happens in meetings. The researchsource 1 says otherwise, and so does my own experience of how deals are won and lost. The buying committee does its real work internally: comparing notes, arguing risk, building the business case, retelling your pitch to people who weren’t in the room. Your deal is decided in conversations your team doesn’t attend.

Narrative Systems

That reframes what messaging is for. The job of your narrative isn’t to persuade the person in the demo. It’s to survive being repeated by that person — imperfectly, from memory, under challenge — to a CFO who has never heard of you.

What survives retelling

Language that survives the internal argument has a shape:

  • One nameable problem. Not a category of pain — a specific condition the champion can point to in their own operation and name in one phrase.
  • A mechanism, not a promise. “They install X, which produces Y” retells cleanly. “They help us grow faster” dissolves on contact with a skeptic.
  • An answer to the risk question. The committee’s real question is rarely “is this good?” It’s “what happens if this fails, and will it be my fault?” Narratives that pre-answer it travel further.

Your narrative isn’t written for the person in the demo. It’s written for the person they have to convince.

Instrumenting the 83%

You can’t attend the internal argument, but you can equip and instrument it. Equip: give the champion artifacts built for forwarding — one-page arguments, not decks; numbers with sources, not adjectives. Instrument: watch for the traces the internal argument leaves — who else from the account starts visiting, which other roles at the account start engaging, what objection shows up in the second call that nobody raised in the first.

What to look for

By the second call, can your champion state the mechanism unaided, in their own words? In my experience, when they can, the internal argument tends to go well. When they can’t, more follow-up rarely rescues it.

The 17% window is where you earn the right to be argued for. The 83% is where you win. Build for the 83%.
